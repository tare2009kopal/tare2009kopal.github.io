A little about me

I’m a student at Dublin High School exploring computational biology and bioengineering. At Stanford, I investigated how choosing a reference genome changes the variants a pipeline finds. At UCI, I helped build a head-tilt mouse controller. I’m drawn to questions where the technical details matter to the people using the result.

That same curiosity shows up in what I teach. Through Bee Stars, I help students recognize patterns in language; through Biology Club and SWENext, I help students try science for themselves. Outside that, you’ll find me cheering at the pool, making music with my sister, or trying one more cube solve.

STANFORD SIMR · SUMMER 2026

REFRAME

Reference-Genome Reassessment for Accurate Mutation Evaluation

Built a variant-calling pipeline on Stanford’s Sherlock cluster to compare 42 samples across hg19, hg38, and T2T-CHM13. Used BWA-MEM and DeepVariant to investigate reference bias under Seraphina Shi and Dr. Mohammad Shahrokh Esfahani. Selected as the sole Cancer Biology/Neuroscience Institute lightning-talk representative and one of three SIMR Award for Excellence recipients.

A closer look

Working with Junming “Seraphina” Shi and Dr. Mohammad Shahrokh Esfahani, I analyzed three related whole-genome samples and processed 39 sarcoma samples. Mapping rates exceeded 99.7%, yet variant calls differed across references. More than 70% of variants were shared; persistent reference-specific differences remained in complex regions.

I received the SIMR Award for Excellence and represented the Cancer Biology/Neuroscience Institute at the cohort presentation.

STANFORD AIMI · SUMMER 2025

Stanford AIMI · pneumonia detection

Built a pneumonia classifier for resource-limited hospitals under Stanford researcher Eva Prakash. Our model achieved 0.911 AUC and won top model accuracy and presentation awards. Testing WhatsApp-compressed X-rays reduced AUC to 0.67, highlighting the importance of evaluating medical AI on real-world inputs.

A closer look

Our best model reached an AUC of 0.911. Performance dropped to 0.67 on low-quality images compressed through WhatsApp—a reminder to evaluate models under the conditions in which people may actually use them.
